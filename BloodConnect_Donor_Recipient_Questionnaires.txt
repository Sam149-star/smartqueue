BLOODCONNECT BLOOD DONOR AND RECIPIENT QUESTIONNAIRES India |
Category-wise forms

PURPOSE This document contains separate 15-question questionnaires for
blood donors (senders) and blood recipients (receivers) across four
profile categories: 1. Senior citizens (60 years and above) 2. Male
adults (18–59 years) 3. Female adults (18–59 years) 4. Minors (below 18
years)

Terminology: - Sender = blood donor - Receiver = blood recipient /
person for whom blood is requested - For minors, the receiver form must
be completed by a parent or legal guardian. - Minors must not be
registered as eligible blood donors in India. The minor donor enquiry
questions below are informational only and must not create a donor
listing.

============================================================ 1. SENIOR
CITIZENS (60 YEARS AND ABOVE)
============================================================

A. DONOR QUESTIONNAIRE — 15 QUESTIONS

1.  What is your full name?
2.  What is your date of birth and current age?
3.  What is your gender?
4.  What is your blood group (A+, A-, B+, B-, AB+, AB-, O+, O-)?
5.  What is your current residential address?
6.  Which city, district and PIN code do you belong to?
7.  What is your mobile number?
8.  Do you have any government-issued identity proof?
9.  Have you donated blood previously?
10. If yes, when was your last blood donation?
11. Are you a first-time donor or a regular donor?
12. Do you have diabetes, hypertension, heart disease or any other
    chronic condition?
13. Are you currently taking any prescribed medication?
14. Have you undergone any major surgery, hospitalisation or blood
    transfusion recently?
15. Are you willing to undergo medical screening at a registered blood
    centre before donating?

B. RECIPIENT QUESTIONNAIRE — 15 QUESTIONS

1.  What is the patient’s full name?
2.  What is the patient’s date of birth and age?
3.  What is the patient’s gender?
4.  What is the patient’s blood group, if known?
5.  What blood component is required (whole blood, red blood cells,
    platelets or plasma)?
6.  How many units are required?
7.  What is the medical reason for requiring blood?
8.  Is the patient currently admitted to a hospital?
9.  What is the hospital’s name?
10. What is the hospital’s complete address and location?
11. What is the treating doctor’s name or department?
12. What is the required date and time for blood availability?
13. Is this an emergency or a scheduled transfusion?
14. Who is the primary family member or caregiver responsible for
    coordination?
15. Can you upload a hospital-issued blood requisition or prescription?

============================================================ 4. MINORS
(BELOW 18 YEARS)
============================================================

IMPORTANT DONATION RULE In India, individuals below 18 years of age are
not eligible to donate blood. The donor-side questions below are for
information and education only. Do not allow a minor to create an active
donor profile, mark themselves available, or be matched with a
recipient.

A. DONOR ENQUIRY QUESTIONNAIRE — 15 QUESTIONS (INFORMATION ONLY — NOT A
DONOR REGISTRATION FORM)

1.  What is your full name?
2.  What is your date of birth?
3.  What is your current age?
4.  What is your gender?
5.  What is your city?
6.  What is your PIN code?
7.  What is your parent’s or legal guardian’s name?
8.  What is your guardian’s mobile number?
9.  Are you aware that blood donation in India requires the donor to be
    at least 18 years old?
10. Are you trying to register as a donor or simply seeking information?
11. Would you like information about voluntary blood donation?
12. Would you like to learn how to support blood donation campaigns?
13. Would your parent or guardian like to receive information about
    blood donation?
14. Would you like a reminder to check donation eligibility after
    turning 18?
15. Do you understand that you cannot be listed as an available blood
    donor until you meet the legal age requirement?

B. RECIPIENT QUESTIONNAIRE — 15 QUESTIONS (TO BE COMPLETED BY A PARENT
OR LEGAL GUARDIAN)

1.  What is the minor patient’s full name?
2.  What is the patient’s date of birth?
3.  What is the patient’s current age?
4.  What is the patient’s gender?
5.  What is the patient’s blood group, if known?
6.  What blood component is required?
7.  How many units are required?
8.  What is the medical condition requiring transfusion?
9.  Is the child currently admitted to a hospital?
10. What is the hospital’s name?
11. What is the hospital’s complete address?
12. What is the treating paediatrician’s or doctor’s name?
13. When is the blood required?
14. What is the parent’s or legal guardian’s full name and mobile
    number?
15. Can the guardian provide the hospital-issued blood requisition and
    applicable consent documentation?

============================================================ IMPORTANT
PLATFORM AND SAFETY NOTES
============================================================

1.  Age and gender should be stored as separate profile attributes. A
    person should not be forced into overlapping categories solely based
    on gender or age.
2.  A senior citizen may be male or female. Use age to determine the
    senior-citizen pathway and gender only where a medically relevant
    question is required.
3.  Do not treat a completed questionnaire as medical clearance. Final
    donor eligibility must be determined by the registered blood
    centre’s medical officer after the required screening.
4.  Do not allow minors to register as active donors.
5.  Confirm the patient’s blood group and component requirement through
    the treating hospital or blood centre. Do not rely only on
    self-reported information.
6.  Do not publicly display donor phone numbers, identity documents or
    precise locations. Share contact details only through a
    consent-based, controlled process.
7.  Collect only information necessary for matching, verification and
    coordination. Sensitive medical answers and identity documents must
    have restricted access.
8.  Use hospital-issued requisitions and coordinate transfusions through
    licensed blood centres. A platform should connect people, not
    independently approve or arrange clinical transfusions.
